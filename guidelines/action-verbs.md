# Action Verb Bank

> Strong verbs to lead resume bullets, grouped by claim type. Purpose: grab the right verb fast and vary across the resume — **without** drifting into weak synonyms just to avoid repeating a good one. Two "Built"s beat one "Built" + one "Composed."
>
> **The verb is ~10% of the bullet.** Its real job is to force a metric or outcome out of you — that's *why* "Reduced / Cut / Accelerated" work: they feel broken without a number after them. A perfect verb on a bullet with no figure still loses to a plain verb with one. When choosing, pick the verb that feels incomplete until you add the result.
>
> **"Technical readers:" verbs** land with an engineering hiring manager but go invisible (or mildly alienating) to a recruiter, agency screener, or ATS. Fine to use — just don't let them carry your top 2–3 bullets, which have to survive a non-technical first pass.

## Built / Shipped

Built, Shipped, Launched, Deployed, Implemented, Engineered, Developed, Released, Rolled out, Delivered
*Technical readers:* Productionized

## Led / Owned

Led, Directed, Owned, Drove, Headed, Championed
*Use sparingly (AI-overused):* Spearheaded, Orchestrated
*Big claim — literal only:* Founded (a company, org, or standing initiative — not a class project or one-off)

## Improved / Optimized

*Incomplete without a metric — these are the number-forcing verbs, use the pressure.*
Reduced, Cut, Accelerated, Sped up, Scaled, Optimized, Refactored, Tuned, Improved
*Technical readers:* Hardened, Stabilized

## Analyzed / Investigated

Analyzed, Investigated, Diagnosed, Modeled, Forecasted, Quantified, Audited, Profiled, Benchmarked, Measured

## Designed / Architected

Designed, Architected, Authored, Established, Defined

## Migrated / Modernized

Migrated, Modernized, Consolidated, Upgraded, Replaced, Retired
*Technical readers:* Replatformed, Rearchitected, Decomposed

## Automated / Integrated

Automated, Scripted, Integrated
*Technical readers:* Provisioned
*(Write "built tooling," not "Tooled.")*

## Mentored / Collaborated

Mentored, Coached, Trained, Onboarded, Advised, Partnered, Collaborated, Coordinated, Guided

## Managed / Operated

*Steady-state work — signals upkeep, not achievement. Pair with scope or a metric.*
Managed, Operated, Ran, Administered, Maintained, Monitored, Oversaw
*("Managed a team" reads as leadership; "Managed a process" reads as maintenance — know which one your bullet is.)*

## Verbs to AVOID

Passive, vague, or lifted straight from a job description:

- "Responsible for"
- "Duties included"
- "Worked on"
- "Helped" / "Helped with"
- "Assisted" / "Assisted with"
- "Participated in"
- "Involved in"
- "Handled"
- "Dealt with"
- "Was tasked with" / "Tasked with"
- "Was in charge of"
- "Utilized" → use **used**
- "Leveraged" → use **used**

## Weak but Not Wrong

Not passive or dishonest — just soft. Upgrade when you can:

- "Created" → Built, Developed, Engineered
- "Improved" (used alone) → attach a number, or switch to Reduced / Cut / Accelerated
- "Made" / "Did" → a sharper verb almost always exists

## LLM-Overused Verbs

Real verbs, but they flag AI-generated resumes. If any appears in 3+ bullets on one resume, swap some out:

- Spearheaded
- Orchestrated
- Pioneered
- Revolutionized
- Transformed (when unwarranted)
- Cultivated
- Enhanced
- Facilitated
- Fostered
- Empowered
- Elevated
- Streamlined (also metric-hiding — needs a number if kept)

## Tense Rules

- **Current role**: present tense ("Lead", "Build", "Own") OR past tense ("Led", "Built", "Owned") — pick one and stay consistent across the whole role
- **Past roles**: past tense, always
- **Within a single role, never mix tenses**
