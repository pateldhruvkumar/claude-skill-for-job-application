# Resume Bullet Formula — XYZ, Done Credibly

> Every bullet earns its place by clearing **two** bars, not one:
> 1. It follows the XYZ structure.
> 2. Every claim in it is one you can **defend out loud in an interview.**
>
> A number you can't defend hurts you more than no number at all. One inflated metric makes a hiring manager quietly discount every honest number on the page.

---

## The Core Formula

> **"Accomplished [X], as measured by [Y], by doing [Z]."**

- **X** = the outcome — what *changed* (not what you were assigned)
- **Y** = the measurement — how much it changed (quantified when the number is real, qualified when it isn't)
- **Z** = the mechanism — *how* you did it (the tech, method, or approach)

> **The principle that makes this work: Z is what makes Y believable.**
> A number with no mechanism reads as a *claim*. The same number with a concrete "how" reads as a *result*. Never write a Y you can't back with a real Z.

---

## The Order in a Bullet

Standard (mechanism last):
```
[Strong action verb] [the X] [the Y] by [the Z].
```

Impact-first (metric leads):
```
[Y impact metric] by [Z method] — [action verb + X].
```

---

## Step-by-Step: Writing One Bullet

1. **Open with an owner verb, not a participant verb.** Use *Built, Led, Designed, Automated, Reduced, Shipped, Owned.* Avoid *Responsible for, Worked on, Helped with, Assisted, Contributed to* — they signal you were near the work, not driving it.
2. **State the outcome (X).** Describe what changed as a result of you, not the task on your job description.
3. **Add the measurement (Y).** A real number, or — when no honest number exists — a concrete qualifier (`across 4 product lines`, `first of its kind for the team`). Never invent one.
4. **Attach the mechanism (Z).** This step is non-negotiable. The specific "how" is what a reader can't fake and what makes your number credible. If you can't name the how in a few real words, the bullet isn't ready.
5. **Cut to one idea, two lines maximum.** One accomplishment per bullet. No semicolons stitching two achievements together.
6. **Run the two checks below before you keep it.**

---

## The Two Non-Negotiable Checks

### Check 1 — Defensibility (run on every bullet)

Ask yourself: *"In an interview, can I explain in one sentence how I arrived at this number?"*

- **Yes** → keep the number.
- **No** → replace it with a qualifier, or cut the metric entirely.

Suspiciously clean figures (90%, 50%, 100%) attached to short stints or small teams are the first thing an experienced reader circles. If the number is real, keep it *and* show the mechanism. If it's a guess, it's a liability.

### Check 2 — Consistency (run across the whole resume + LinkedIn)

This is the mistake that can end a process before an interview even happens: the **same job showing up with different titles or dates** across your tailored versions.

- **One canonical job title per role**, everywhere.
- **One canonical date range per role**, everywhere — including LinkedIn.
- **One consistent format** for dates, technology names, and capitalization (`Jul 2025`, not `Jul.` in one place and `July` in another).

Recruiters cross-reference. Two versions that disagree read as embellishment.

---

## Quantify Honestly — Coverage Is Not the Goal

> **Ignore any "quantify 60% of bullets" quota.** Chasing number density is exactly what produces inflated, indefensible metrics. The goal is **credible impact, not a number in every line.**

- **Three defensible numbers beat eight inflated ones.** One implausible metric taxes the credibility of every real one around it.
- Use a **number** when it's real and you can source it.
- Use a **qualifier** when you can't — this is honest and fully acceptable, not a failure.
- Never round up to a clean figure just to have one.

---

## One Bullet = One Accomplishment

- **No semicolon splices.** `...cut latency 35%; collaborated cross-functionally to translate requirements` is two bullets pretending to be one — and the second half is a duty with no result. Split it or cut it.
- **No trailing duty clauses.** If the tail end has no measurable outcome, it's filler.
- **No 3–4 stacked `and… and… and…` clauses.** Pick the strongest one or two; drop the rest.

---

## Match the Number to the Claim

The metric must measure the thing the bullet is actually about.

- ❌ `Built dashboards that cut ad-hoc requests 60% and enabled tracking for 150+ event attendees.`
  The attendee count doesn't measure the dashboard's impact — it's a scale number bolted on. It reads as padding and makes the real 60% look inflated too.
- ✓ `Built self-service dashboards that cut recurring ad-hoc data requests ~60% and reduced report refresh time 13%.`
  Both numbers describe what the dashboards actually did.

---

## Good Examples (and why each number is believable)

✓ "Reduced API p99 latency 47% (850ms → 450ms) by introducing Redis caching and rewriting the hot-path query in Rust."
- X = reduced latency · Y = 47%; 850ms → 450ms · Z = Redis caching + Rust rewrite
- *Believable because:* the before/after and the named mechanism make the number checkable.

✓ "Led 4-engineer team migrating a monolith to event-driven microservices on Kubernetes, shipping in 6 months under budget."
- X = led migration · Y = 4 engineers, 6 months, under budget · Z = event-driven microservices on Kubernetes
- *Believable because:* team size and timeline are concrete, verifiable facts.

✓ "Cut new-hire onboarding time 60% (5 days → 2 days) by automating environment setup with Terraform and writing 12 runbooks."
- X = cut onboarding time · Y = 60%; 5 → 2 days · Z = Terraform + 12 runbooks

✓ "Shipped MVP for a new billing platform serving 50k customers in Q1 by partnering with finance and security and choosing Stripe over an in-house build."
- X = shipped MVP · Y = 50k customers in Q1 · Z = cross-functional partnership + Stripe decision

✓ "Improved test-suite pass rate 71% → 98% by identifying race conditions and replacing setTimeout with condition-based waiting across 230 tests."
- *Believable because:* the mechanism (what was fixed, in how many tests) explains the jump.

## Bad Examples (and the specific failure)

❌ "Responsible for backend development."
- No X, no Y, no Z. Pure duty description.

❌ "Worked on improving performance."
- Weak passive verb, no number, no method.

❌ "Improved system performance by 90%."
- **Inflated, no mechanism.** A huge round number with nothing to back it — the classic indefensible metric a reader rounds down to zero.

❌ "Delivered a scalable data pipeline solution at scale."
- **Filler stacked on filler.** "Solution" and "at scale" say nothing concrete. Name the tech and the result instead.

❌ "Cut latency 35%; collaborated cross-functionally to translate requirements into production software."
- **Two accomplishments spliced together,** and the second is a duty with no measurement.

❌ "Leveraged synergies to optimize stakeholder outcomes."
- Filler verbs, no X/Y/Z. A textbook AI-tell.

❌ "Helped the team migrate to Kubernetes."
- "Helped" understates your contribution; no measurement, no specifics.

❌ "Successfully delivered a project on time."
- "Successfully" and "on time" are filler with no X, Y, or Z.

❌ *Vault AI — "Software Engineer" (Jul 2024–Present)* on one version, *"AI Engineer" (Jul 2025–Oct 2025)* on another.
- **A consistency failure**, not a wording one — the same role must read identically everywhere.

---

## Filler & AI-Tell Words to Cut

Delete on sight and replace with a concrete noun or number:

`at scale` · `solution` · `pixel-perfect` · `leveraged` · `synergies` · `successfully` · `seamlessly` · `robust` · `cutting-edge` · `world-class` · `state-of-the-art` · `responsible for` · `worked on` · `helped` · `enabling data-driven [anything]` · `cross-functionally` (when it carries no real detail)

---

## Quantification Sources (when a number is real but hard to find)

- **Percentage change** — "reduced X by 47%"
- **Absolute change** — "from 850ms to 450ms"
- **Scale** — "for 50k users / 12 services / 4-person team"
- **Time** — "shipped in 3 weeks / within the first quarter"
- **Currency** — "saved $2M annually / managed a $500k budget"
- **Quality** — "test coverage 60% → 95%"
- **Adoption** — "adopted by 6 of 9 teams"
- **Comparison** — "first in company history / 2x faster than the previous approach"

---

## When You Genuinely Can't Quantify

Qualifying is honest and effective — not a fallback failure:

- "across 4 product lines"
- "adopted org-wide"
- "first system of its kind at the company"
- "owned end-to-end"

Use a qualifier rather than manufacturing a number. A credible qualitative claim always beats a fabricated statistic.

---

## Final Per-Bullet Checklist

Run this quick yes/no gate before any bullet ships. Every answer must be **yes.**

- [ ] Starts with an **owner** action verb (not *responsible for / worked on / helped*)?
- [ ] States an **outcome**, not just a task?
- [ ] The number (or qualifier) is **real** and I can **defend it in one sentence**?
- [ ] The **mechanism (Z)** is concrete and specific?
- [ ] **One** accomplishment, **≤2 lines**, no semicolon splice?
- [ ] The number actually **measures what the bullet is about**?
- [ ] The **title, dates, and tech names match** every other version and my LinkedIn?
